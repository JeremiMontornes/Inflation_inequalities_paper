\section{Individual Inflation Dispersion Across COICOP Digits}
\label{app:aggregation-bias}

Table~\ref{tab:aggregation-bias-excluding-rents-harmonized} evaluates how product aggregation
affects the individual P20--P80 inflation range. Household inflation is recomputed at each available
COICOP digit depth from fixed 2020 HBS baskets and annual expenditure-margin RAS weights. For
Malta at digit 5, relative-expenditure weights are used because household RAS does not converge.
Actual and imputed rents (HBS 041 and 042) and the corresponding HICP actual-rent component are excluded
before product aggregation. France, Italy and Spain use the dedicated digit-4 household files;
Germany is available through digit 3, while the remaining countries use their finest available
detail. Portugal is excluded because its detailed household expenditure fields are unavailable.
The resulting sample therefore contains 19 countries and does not require household income.

\paragraph{Inflation and aggregation bias.}
Let $I^d_{i,y,m}$ denote the chained household index for household $i$ at digit depth $d$.
Inflation in 2022 is calculated from annual-average indices,
\begin{equation}
 \pi^d_{i,2022}=100\left(
 \frac{\frac{1}{12}\sum_{m=1}^{12} I^d_{i,2022,m}}
 {\frac{1}{12}\sum_{m=1}^{12} I^d_{i,2021,m}}-1\right).
\end{equation}
For each country, $G^d=P_{80}(\pi^d_i)-P_{20}(\pi^d_i)$, where the percentiles use HBS survey
weights. Aggregation bias is $B^{d,d'}=G^d-G^{d'}$ for $d'>d$; a negative value therefore indicates
that the finer product classification reveals a wider household-inflation range. Because matched
coverage can vary with digit depth, the comparison can also reflect changes in product support.
