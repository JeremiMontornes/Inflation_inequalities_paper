\section{Aggregation Bias Across COICOP Digits}
\label{app:aggregation-bias}

Table~\ref{tab:aggregation-bias-excluding-rents-harmonized} compares inflation gaps across COICOP
digit depths using HBS microdata and reports a digit-3 benchmark from Eurostat national-quintile HBS aggregates.
Macro D3 excl. and all microdata columns exclude actual and imputed rents (HBS 041 and 042)
and the corresponding HICP actual-rent component (041), and use RAS-calibrated weights.
All columns use annual-average chained-index inflation. The macro benchmark is recalculated
with RAS; the rent-robustness table retains its relative-expenditure specification.

\paragraph{Micro--macro consistency.}
We first reaggregate the 2020 household records into national income quintiles and compare
survey-weighted expenditure shares with Eurostat's published HBS shares on a common digit-3
support, excluding rents. Ranking households by total net household income substantially improves
this match relative to ranking them by equivalised income. Across products and quintiles, the mean
absolute difference in budget shares falls from 0.20 to 0.05 percentage points in the Netherlands
and from 0.31 to 0.04 in Belgium; the corresponding figures for France are 0.19 and 0.03.
This comparison precedes HICP calibration and therefore identifies a difference in the construction
of income groups rather than an effect of the inflation-index weighting method. We use total
household income for the microdata quintiles throughout this appendix.

\paragraph{Baskets and weights.}
Microdata quintiles are defined separately within each country using survey-weighted net household
income without division by an equivalence scale (total household income). The sample covers 18 euro-area countries. Fifteen countries provide expenditure variables
through digit 5; Germany, France and Spain provide digit 3 in the local microdata files.
Italy is excluded because household income is unavailable, and Portugal because its detailed
expenditure columns are unpopulated. The 2020 microdata expenditure baskets are reconstructed at each digit depth from
the same finest available expenditure variables after removing rents. Nonpositive or missing
expenditure entries contribute zero, while eligible households remain in the survey denominators.

For product $j$ in quintile $q$, let $\bar{x}_{qj}$ be survey-weighted mean HBS expenditure,
$\bar{x}_j$ the national mean, $s_{q,y}$ the package's consumption share of quintile $q$,
and $w^{HICP}_{j,y}$ the HICP product share on matched support. RAS iteratively rescales
rows and columns of the positive seed
$\max\{s_{q,y}w^{HICP}_{j,y}\bar{x}_{qj}/\bar{x}_j,10^{-12}\}$.
The calibrated expenditure masses $M_{qj,y}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \sum_j M_{qj,y}=s_{q,y},\qquad
 \sum_q M_{qj,y}=w^{HICP}_{j,y},\qquad
 \omega_{qj,y}=M_{qj,y}/s_{q,y}.
\end{equation}
Both the macro and micro calculations use the package's published consumption shares by
income quintile, selecting the latest available wave preceding each weight year, and annual
HICP product margins. These quintile margins are not re-estimated from the matched microdata
at each digit depth. A shared ECOICOP1 correspondence table defines the D2--D5 mappings for both the
household-index function and this appendix. Expenditures and historical HICP prices
use the same canonical product codes; rents are removed before D2 aggregation.
Micro expenditures are aggregated by quintile before RAS calibration;
the table does not average separately chained household indices.
Macro baskets use the corresponding published Eurostat HBS expenditures, selecting the most
recent available preceding wave for each matched item. Micro prices
use the archived ECOICOP1 HICP indices and item weights, which match the HBS classification,
and are reaggregated with annual HICP weights. An undivided terminal HICP class is matched
to its identical HBS code padded with zeros (for example, electricity 0451/04510
and hospital services 063/06300). Five additional terminal correspondences for hospital
services and education are validated against the HBS manual and Eurostat labels and
recorded in an explicit mapping table. Each corresponding published price and weight
enters once; no distinct subclass receives an imputed parent price. Fuzzy label matching
is used to propose candidates, and unvalidated candidates are excluded.
Rent components are removed before constructing the broad housing index. Products without
complete monthly prices or annual weights are excluded; prices are not imputed to distinct
subclasses. Matched expenditure coverage can vary across digit depths, so the reported
differences can combine aggregation and coverage effects. The macro benchmark retains
the price source and classification bridge used in the rent-robustness appendix.

\paragraph{Inflation and aggregation bias.}
Let $I^d_{q,y,m}$ denote the chained Laspeyres index for quintile $q$ at digit depth $d$.
Inflation in 2022 is calculated from annual-average indices,
\begin{equation}
 \pi^d_{q,2022}=100\left(
 \frac{\frac{1}{12}\sum_{m=1}^{12} I^d_{q,2022,m}}
 {\frac{1}{12}\sum_{m=1}^{12} I^d_{q,2021,m}}-1\right).
\end{equation}
The gap is $G^d=\pi^d_{Q1,2022}-\pi^d_{Q5,2022}$ and the microdata aggregation bias is
$B^{d,d'}=G^d-G^{d'}$ for $d'>d$. Thus, negative bias indicates a larger gap at finer digit depth.

The micro--macro comparison also reflects differences in source, matched products and income-group
construction; it should not be interpreted as aggregation bias. The earlier validation of
published HBS expenditure shares uses native digit-3 variables, whereas the inflation
calculations aggregate the finest available expenditure variables within each country.
In Belgium and the Netherlands, these constructions can differ, notably for insurance expenditure.
